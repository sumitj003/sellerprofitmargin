How to Calculate Etsy Profit Margin (2025 Complete Guide)

January 2025 12 min read Beginner friendly

If you sell on Etsy and you're not calculating your profit margin before listing a product, you might be losing money on every single sale without realizing it. This is one of the most common mistakes new Etsy sellers make — they focus on revenue and forget about actual profit.

In this guide, you'll learn exactly how to calculate your Etsy profit margin, what fees to account for, what counts as a healthy margin, and how to use our free calculator to get the answer in seconds.

Quick answer
Etsy Profit Margin = (Selling Price − All Costs) ÷ Selling Price × 100. "All Costs" includes product cost, shipping, Etsy's 6.5% transaction fee, listing fee ($0.20), and payment processing fee (~3% + $0.25).

The Etsy Profit Margin Formula

The profit margin formula is the same for any business, but the costs you need to include on Etsy are specific to the platform. Here is the complete formula:

Profit Margin % = ((Selling Price − Total Costs) ÷ Selling Price) × 100

And Total Costs for Etsy sellers specifically includes:

  • Product cost (COGS): What you paid to make or source the item
  • Shipping cost: Packaging materials + postage
  • Etsy transaction fee: 6.5% of the sale price including shipping
  • Listing fee: $0.20 per listing (renews every 4 months or per sale)
  • Payment processing fee: ~3% + $0.25 per transaction
  • Etsy Ads spend: If you run Etsy Ads, add this cost

Real Example: A $45 Handmade Candle

Let's walk through a complete, realistic example. You sell a handmade soy candle for $45 on Etsy with $5 shipping. Here's what your costs actually look like:

Cost Breakdown — $45 Etsy Candle
Where your $45 actually goes
  • Product cost (wax, wick, jar, fragrance): $8.00
  • Packaging + shipping label: $5.50
  • Etsy transaction fee (6.5% of $50): $3.25
  • Listing fee: $0.20
  • Payment processing (3% + $0.25): $1.60
  • Total Costs: $18.55
  • Net Profit: $45.00 − $18.55 = $26.45
  • Profit Margin: $26.45 ÷ $45 × 100 = 58.8%

That's actually a strong margin. But notice how easily it can erode — add Etsy Ads at $0.50 per click and run 20 clicks to get one sale, and you've added $10 in ad costs, dropping your margin to 36.5%. Still good, but it shows why tracking every cost matters.

Common mistake
Many Etsy sellers forget to include the payment processing fee (3% + $0.25) in their calculations. On a $45 sale that's $1.60 — it adds up to hundreds of dollars per month at scale.

Complete Guide to Etsy Fees in 2025

Etsy has several fees that sellers need to account for. Understanding each one is essential for accurate margin calculation.

Etsy Fee Types (2025)
All the fees Etsy charges sellers

Transaction Fee (6.5%)

This is Etsy's main revenue fee. It applies to the full sale price including whatever shipping charge you pass to the buyer. If you sell an item for $30 with $5 buyer-paid shipping, Etsy charges 6.5% on $35, which is $2.28.

Listing Fee ($0.20)

Every time you list or relist an item, Etsy charges $0.20. Listings stay active for 4 months. If an item sells, the listing automatically renews for another $0.20 per item sold. For high-volume products, this is negligible. For slow movers, it can add up.

Payment Processing Fee (~3% + $0.25)

This fee covers credit card processing and varies slightly by country. For US sellers, it's typically 3% + $0.25 per transaction. This applies to all sales processed through Etsy Payments.

Etsy Ads (Variable)

Etsy Ads (formerly Promoted Listings) are optional but many sellers use them. You set a daily budget and pay per click. Typical CPC ranges from $0.20 to $1.50 depending on the category. Your ad cost per sale depends on your conversion rate.

Offsite Ads Fee (12–15%)

If Etsy promotes your item externally (Google Shopping, Facebook, etc.) and it results in a sale, Etsy charges 15% (or 12% if you earn over $10,000/year). You cannot opt out of this if your annual revenue exceeds $10,000.

What is a Good Profit Margin for Etsy?

Etsy Profit Margin Benchmarks
Where does your margin stand?
  • Below 15%: Danger zone. You're barely covering costs. Any increase in material prices or ad spend will make you unprofitable.
  • 15–25%: Acceptable but tight. You have some cushion but not much room for growth or experimentation.
  • 25–40%: Healthy. This is the target range for most successful Etsy sellers. You can afford to run ads, absorb occasional returns, and still grow.
  • 40%+: Excellent. Typically seen in digital products, printables, and high-value handmade items. This margin lets you scale aggressively.

Digital products on Etsy (printables, digital art, templates) often achieve 70–90% margins because there's no material cost, no shipping cost, and the same file sells unlimited times.

How to Improve Your Etsy Margin

If your margin is below 20%, here are the most effective levers to pull:

1. Increase your prices

This is the most direct lever but the one sellers fear most. Research shows that buyers on Etsy are less price-sensitive than on Amazon — they're paying for handmade, unique, or personalized items. A 10–15% price increase often has minimal impact on conversion rate while significantly improving margin.

2. Reduce materials cost

Buy supplies in bulk, find alternative suppliers, or negotiate better rates. If you're buying wax for candles at $3/lb retail, switching to a wholesale supplier at $1.80/lb immediately improves your margin on every single sale.

3. Optimise your Etsy SEO to reduce ad spend

If you're spending heavily on Etsy Ads to get visibility, improving your organic search ranking means fewer paid clicks needed for the same sales volume — directly improving your margin. Tools like eRank help you find the keywords your buyers actually search, so your listings rank without paying for every click.

4. Kill underperforming Etsy Ads

Review your Etsy Ads data regularly. Cut any ad listings with a ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) below 3x. Keep only listings where ad spend is generating profitable sales.

5. Move to higher-margin products

If a product consistently earns below 20%, consider discontinuing it and focusing on higher-margin items. Not every product deserves space in your shop.

5 Profit Calculation Mistakes Etsy Sellers Make

  1. Forgetting payment processing fees. The 3% + $0.25 Etsy Payments fee is easy to miss because it doesn't appear on the listing — it deducts from your payment.
  2. Not including your own time. If you're making handmade items, your labor has a cost. Even if you don't pay yourself formally, tracking your hourly rate helps you understand if the business is truly profitable.
  3. Using revenue instead of profit for decisions. A shop doing $10,000/month in revenue but only $1,500 in profit is not as successful as it looks.
  4. Ignoring returns and refunds. Build a 1–3% buffer into your pricing to account for items that need refunding or replacing.
  5. Not recalculating when material costs change. Inflation affects your COGS. If your candle wax cost increases 20%, your margin drops — you need to reprice.
